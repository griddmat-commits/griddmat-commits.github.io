BESS model testing: grid-forming and grid-following

Battery projects bring specific modelling challenges: charge and discharge operating points, fast control interactions, very low system strength and, increasingly, grid-forming behaviour. GridDMAT tests BESS models to AEMO, TNSP and US ISO requirements.

Discuss your project

What's different about BESS models

  • Both charging and discharging operating points must be tested
  • Grid-forming inverters behave differently to grid-following ones during faults, phase angle jumps and weak-grid cases
  • Plant controller and inverter interactions can cause oscillations that only show up in EMT studies
  • Firmware updates are frequent, so fast, repeatable re-runs matter

Where GridDMAT helps

Full DMAT suites for NEM batteries, ERCOT model quality tests, commissioning-stage model validation, and model updates after firmware or controller changes.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between grid-forming and grid-following?

A grid-following inverter synchronises to the measured grid voltage and injects current. A grid-forming inverter sets its own voltage and frequency reference and behaves more like a voltage source, which changes its response to faults, phase jumps and weak grids.

Is grid-forming testing different under the DMAT?

The DMAT case list is the same, but grid-forming plants respond differently, especially to phase angle jumps, frequency events and very low system strength, so assessment needs care.
